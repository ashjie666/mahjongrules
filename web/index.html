Independent reference

Mahjong rules, scored and explained

Six rule sets share one tile set and almost nothing else. This handbook shows what actually changes between them — tile counts, minimum hands, claiming rules and payout — so you can sit down at any table and know what you are playing.

Short answer: mahjong is a four-player tile game in which you draw, discard and claim tiles to build four sets plus one pair. A hand is only valid if it also clears the style's minimum — 8 fan in Chinese Official, at least one yaku under riichi rules in Japanese, 5 tai in Taiwanese, and a card-listed pattern in American mahjong.

Chinese styles

Competitive

MCR (Chinese Official)

144 tiles including flowers, a hard 8-fan minimum, and 79 scoring patterns that stack under strict rules.

 Fast

Sichuan mahjong

108 tiles, no honours, a mandatory dropped suit, and blood battle play that continues after the first win.

 Casual

Hong Kong mahjong

The most common mahjong game at English-speaking tables: 13 tiles, chow allowed, and a minimum that depends on house rules.

 16 tiles

Taiwanese mahjong

One extra set changes everything: 16 tiles in hand, five sets plus a pair, and flowers worth one tai each.

Japanese & Western

Popular

Riichi mahjong

Declare riichi when your hand is ready, watch for furiten, and count dora on top of your yaku.

 Card-based

American mahjong (NMJL)

No chow, two Joker types, and a yearly card that defines which hands are legal at all.

 BMJA

British mahjong

One chow per hand, a 14-tile kong box, and a points system that is often confused with EMA ranking.

 3 players

Three-player mahjong

North wind leaves the set, the wall gets shorter, and defence becomes far more important.
