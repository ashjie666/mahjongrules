Mahjong vs Mahjong Solitaire

Searching for mahjong usually returns a tile-matching game. It uses the same tiles and none of the same rules.

ComparisonLast reviewed: 30 October 2026~4 min read

At a glance

Players
One, against a layout — not four against each other
Tiles used
The same 144-tile images, no wall, no draws
Goal
Clear the layout by pairing identical free tiles
Related to mahjong?
In artwork only. None of the rules carry over

The game you found is not the game you wanted

Search for "mahjong" and most results are mahjong solitaire: a single-player game where you remove pairs of tiles from a stacked layout until the board is clear. It uses the same tile designs and the same name, and it shares no rules with the four-player game.

This matters because people who have never played either game often confuse the two. If you are here to learn mahjong, you want the four-player game. If you are here for the tile-matching puzzle, you want the solitaire game. They are different hobbies that happen to share an artwork style.

What mahjong solitaire actually is

Solitaire, also called tile matching or Shanghai, lays out a pattern of tiles in layers. A tile can only be removed when it is "free": nothing on top of it and at least one side clear. You remove two identical free tiles at a time. When no legal pair remains, the game is over.

The skill is planning, not rules. Good players work from the top down, remove tiles that block the most options, and avoid taking a pair that leaves a lone tile stranded.

The table makes the point: every row differs. There is no rule of the four-player game that survives into solitaire, and no rule of solitaire that survives into the four-player game.

Why the names collided

The tile-matching game was popularised in the 1980s under the mahjong name because the tiles were already recognisable and the layout looked like the square wall built at the start of a real hand. The borrowing was visual, and it stuck.

The consequence is a persistent search problem. People looking for rules find solitaire; people looking for solitaire find rulebooks. Both groups leave disappointed, which is why this page exists.

Terminology that gives it away

If a page mentions three or four players, walls, discards, claims, fan or tai, it is describing the real game. If it mentions layouts, layers, free tiles, undo or a timer, it is describing solitaire. A single sentence is usually enough to tell which game you are reading about.

Quick answers

Is mahjong solitaire a mahjong variant?

No. It is a separate single-player tile-matching game. It shares the tile artwork and nothing else.

Can you play real mahjong alone?

Only in a training sense. Some apps provide an AI table for one human and three bots, which is the closest single-player equivalent of the real game.

Does learning solitaire help with mahjong?

Only for tile recognition. You will learn the suits and the dragon and wind faces, and nothing about the rules.
